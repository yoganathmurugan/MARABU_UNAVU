Our Story

Food made slowly. Memories served warmly. Tamil heritage brought back to the table.

Why We Started This

Over the last 100–200 years, industrialization, the Green Revolution, and modern packaged food have pushed hundreds of nutritious South Indian dishes out of everyday use. Foods that were once daily staples in Tamil households — heritage millets, palmyra-based preparations, forgotten festival sweets, medicinal home remedies — are now unfamiliar to most people under 40, especially in cities.

Marabu Unavu exists to close that gap, the way a modern e-commerce brand would: by presenting these foods professionally packaged, described, priced, and sold — so the product itself becomes the teaching tool. Someone browsing this site should leave knowing foods they'd never heard of before they arrived.

How We Choose What to Sell

Every item in the collection is grounded in real, documented South Indian culinary history — heritage millets, Sangam-era temple foods, Tam-Brahm home recipes, palmyra-based preparations — researched from published food-heritage sources, not invented for the catalog. Each product page pairs what the food is with why it quietly disappeared from most kitchens, so browsing the shop doubles as a short lesson in food history.

What You'll Find Here

17 heritage items spanning four categories — Millet Staples, Forgotten Snacks, Festival & Travel Sweets, and Forgotten Ingredients & Remedies — mapped across five Tamil Nadu culinary regions. Browse by category, explore by region, or read through how each item was traditionally made on the Processing page.

A Note on What This Is

Marabu Unavu is an academic coursework project built for OFD352 — Traditional Indian Foods. It's a fully working front-end shopping experience — browsing, wishlist, cart, and checkout all function — but no real payments are processed and no real orders ship. It's a teaching tool wearing the shape of a real e-commerce brand.
